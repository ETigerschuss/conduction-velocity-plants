# Which study plants produce action potentials vs variation potentials?

Literature review (parallel-agent research; the adversarial-verification pass did not
complete due to a token limit, so treat citations as researched-but-unverified pending a
final check). Key point: the SAME species can produce both — the **stimulus** decides.
Non-damaging (touch/cold/excision) -> action potential; **wounding (flame/burn) -> variation
potential**. This study used a **flame** stimulus, so most recordings are expected to be
variation potentials, except the rapid-movement plants (Venus flytrap, Mimosa) whose sharp
signals are genuine APs.

## Solanum lycopersicum (tomato; syn. Lycopersicon esculentum, cv. Moneymaker)
- **AP:** yes — DEMONSTRATED. Non-damaging excision/cutting of an intact (unwounded) leaf evokes a fast action potential; wound-triggered AP-like depolarizations of 79 ± 3.3 mV recorded specifically in sieve-tube elements and companion cells (smaller 23 ± 1.6 mV signals in other cells). Rhodes, Thain & Wildon 1996 Planta; 1999 Ann Bot.
- **VP:** yes — DEMONSTRATED. Flame/heat wounding of a leaf or cotyledon evokes a slow, wound-induced variation potential that propagates systemically through the vasculature and drives systemic proteinase-inhibitor (pin2) gene induction. Wildon et al. 1992 Nature; Rhodes, Thain & Wildon 1996 Planta; 1999 Ann Bot.
- **Stimulus:** Clear and experimentally dissociated: NON-DAMAGING stimulus (excision/cut of an intact leaf; also cold/cold-shock reported for the AP class) -> action potential. WOUNDING (flame/heat, crush) -> variation potential. Diagnostic result: excising a leaf ~90 s after flame-wounding produces an AP superimposed on the VP, showing the two are physically distinct, separately triggerable signals (Rhodes, Thain & Wildon 1999). Both signals can induce systemic transcript accumulation.
- *Cite:* Wildon DC, Thain JF, Minchin PEH, Gubb IR, Reilly AJ, Skipper YD, Doherty HM, O'Donnell PJ, Bowles DJ (1992) 'Electrical signalling and systemic proteinase inhibitor induction in the wounded plant.' Nature 360:62-65. | Rhodes JD, Thain JF, Wildon DC (1996) 'The pathway for systemic electrical signal conduction in the wounded tomato plant.' Planta 200:50-57. | Rhodes JD, Thain JF, Wildon DC (1999) 'Evidence for physically distinct systemic signalling pathways in the wounded tomato plant.' Annals of Botany 84:109-116. | (also: Volkov & Shtessel 2018 Bioelectrochemistry 124:195-205 show electrical excitability/electrotonic coupling within and between tomato plants via electrostimulation — supporting excitability but electrotonic, not the classic wound AP/VP.)

## Capsicum (chili peppers; C. annuum)
- **AP:** unknown — NOT demonstrated. No primary plant-electrophysiology study showing a propagating, all-or-none action potential in Capsicum was found. Existing 'electrical signal' work on pepper measures stem internal electrical conductivity (impedance), not membrane depolarizations. Plausible/likely by analogy to its close Solanaceae relative tomato, but this is inference, not evidence.
- **VP:** unknown — NOT demonstrated. No study reports a wound-induced variation/slow-wave potential in Capsicum. Likely present given the tomato precedent and shared Solanaceae vasculature, but undemonstrated in the primary literature located.
- **Stimulus:** Not established for AP/VP. Published Capsicum electrophysiology characterizes stem internal electrical conductivity (ECps) / plant-induced electrical signal (PIES) under temperature, salinity, drought, and urea-fertilizer treatments — i.e. tissue impedance reflecting ion/water transport, a fundamentally different quantity from wound- or touch-evoked propagating AP/VP. No touch/cold->AP or wound/burn->VP dependence has been tested or shown.
- *Cite:* Kim HN, Seok YJ, Park GM, et al. (2023) 'Monitoring of plant-induced electrical signal of pepper plants (Capsicum annuum L.) according to urea fertilizer application.' Scientific Reports 13:291 — impedance/conductivity (PIES), NOT AP/VP. | Kim et al. (2021) 'Early detection of plant stress using the internal electrical conductivity of Capsicum annuum in response to temperature and salinity stress.' Plant Growth Regulation (doi:10.1007/s10725-021-00747-z) — internal electrical conductivity (ECps), NOT AP/VP. No AP/VP demonstration located for any Capsicum species.

## Ocimum basilicum (basil) — Lamiaceae
- **AP:** unknown — no non-damaging AP-eliciting stimulus (touch, cold, mild current) was ever tested on basil, so an all-or-none action potential has not been demonstrated. (A separate non-peer-reviewed arXiv preprint, 'Plant Bioelectric Early Warning Systems,' logs basil electrode signals but makes non-credible human-emotion-communication claims and does NOT demonstrate a classical AP.)
- **VP:** likely/yes — a flame (burning) stimulus applied to a basil leaf evoked a slow, decremental electrophysiological response the authors explicitly call a 'putative wound potential' (i.e. variation/wound-potential class), with a ~3–6 s delay and cross-species conduction velocities of ~2–9 mm/s consistent with VP, not AP. Authors hedge with 'putative'; no ion-channel/pharmacology confirmation was done.
- **Stimulus:** flame/burning (damaging) → slow wound/variation-type potential (demonstrated). No AP-eliciting (touch/cold) stimulus was tested, so AP behavior is undetermined.
- *Cite:* Madariaga D. et al. (2024) 'A library of electrophysiological responses in plants — a model of transversal education and open science.' Plant Signaling & Behavior 19(1):2310977. doi:10.1080/15592324.2024.2310977 (PubMed 38493508). Basil = Ocimum basilicum, flame stimulus.

## Plectranthus (P. purpuratus, 'Argentinian dollar') — Lamiaceae
- **AP:** unknown — only a flame stimulus was applied; no touch/cold test, so no all-or-none action potential demonstrated. No classic AP study of Plectranthus/Coleus was located in the literature.
- **VP:** likely — the same study used P. purpuratus as its worked setup example, recording a flame-evoked 'putative wound potential' (variation/wound-potential class). NOTE: evidence is for the species P. purpuratus, which stands in for the genus; the specific Plectranthus species you may have in mind was not individually tested.
- **Stimulus:** flame/burning (damaging) → slow wound/variation-type potential (demonstrated at genus level). AP behavior untested.
- *Cite:* Madariaga D. et al. (2024) 'A library of electrophysiological responses in plants — a model of transversal education and open science.' Plant Signaling & Behavior 19(1):2310977. doi:10.1080/15592324.2024.2310977. Plectranthus purpuratus, flame stimulus.

## Lamiaceae — overall assessment / framework context
- **AP:** no direct demonstration in any of the four taxa. Across angiosperms generally, non-damaging stimuli (cold, touch, mild electrical) elicit fast, all-or-none, phloem-propagated action potentials — but no Lamiaceae species has been used to demonstrate this, and no touch/cold test on these genera exists in the located literature.
- **VP:** yes, for basil and Plectranthus only (flame-evoked 'putative wound potentials', Madariaga 2024). VPs (variation/slow-wave potentials) are the well-established angiosperm response to wounding/burning (xylem-borne hydraulic + chemical signal, decremental, slow ~mm/s). Not electrophysiologically confirmed for Mentha or rosemary.
- **Stimulus:** Established general rule (applied, not species-proven for most of this group): non-damaging (cold/touch) → action potential; damaging (wound/burn/heat) → variation/wound potential. Only the damaging-stimulus→VP arm has direct evidence in this group (basil, Plectranthus).
- *Cite:* Framework reviews: Fromm & Lautner (2007) Plant, Cell & Environment 30:249–257; Sukhov V., Sukhova E., Vodeneev V. (2019) 'Long-distance electrical signals as a link between local stressor action and systemic responses in higher plants.' Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology 146:63–84. doi:10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2018.11.009. Direct Lamiaceae data: Madariaga et al. (2024) Plant Signaling & Behavior 19(1):2310977.

## Fabaceae / Mimosa pudica (sensitive plant)
- **AP:** yes — fast, all-or-none pulvinar/vascular action potential (40–70 mV spike + plateau) propagating ~2 cm/s in stem and up to ~4 cm/s in the pulvinus; drives seismonastic leaflet folding; demonstrated by classic intracellular/extracellular recordings (Sibaoka 1966, 1991; Volkov et al. 2010).
- **VP:** yes — wounding (flame, cutting) evokes a slower, graded (non-all-or-none), decremental variation potential propagated through the xylem/hydraulically that crosses pulvini and reaches distant organs; distinguished from the AP since Houwink (1935) and characterized in later electrophysiology (Hagihara & Toyota 2020 review).
- **Stimulus:** Non-damaging stimuli (touch, chilled-water drop, mild electrical stim) → AP, spatially confined, cannot cross pulvini. Wounding (burn/flame, cut) → variation potential (VP/slow-wave) that crosses pulvini and spreads systemically via xylem, plus accompanying wound-triggered APs. Well demonstrated, not assumed.
- *Cite:* Sibaoka T (1991) 'Rapid plant movements triggered by action potentials' Bot. Mag. Tokyo (J. Plant Res.) 104:73–95; Houwink AL (1935) 'The conduction of excitation in Mimosa pudica' Rec. Trav. Bot. Néerl. 32:51–91 (first distinguishes fast AP from slow/hydraulic wave); Samejima M & Sibaoka T (1980) Plant Cell Physiol. 21:467–479 and (1982) 23:459–465; Volkov AG et al. (2010) 'Mimosa pudica: electrical and mechanical stimulation of plant movements' Plant Cell Environ. 33:163–173; review: Hagihara T & Toyota M (2020) 'Mechanical signaling in the sensitive plant Mimosa pudica L.' Plants 9(5):587 (cites Houwink 1935; Sibaoka 1966; Oda & Abe 1972 for AP/VP).

## Droseraceae / Dionaea muscipula (Venus flytrap)
- **AP:** yes — the canonical, best-characterized plant action potential (~1–2 ms, Ca2+/Na+-based); trigger-hair deflection (>~3°), heat (>~38°C), cold, glutamate, and electrical stimulation all fire propagating APs that drive trap closure. First recorded by Burdon-Sanderson (1873); intracellular characterization by Hodick & Sievers (1988); AP-counting/ion basis by Böhm et al. (2016) and Scherzer/Iosip et al. (2022).
- **VP:** no / developmentally restricted — in MATURE traps wounding evokes the SAME action potentials as trigger-hair stimulation, not classic variation potentials (Pavlovič et al. 2017). A slow-wave/variation-type potential is reported only in electrically IMMATURE (stage V) traps, which switch to firing fast APs once mature (stage VI) (Scherzer/Iosip et al. 2022). A robust adult VP is therefore not the operational signal; existence of a bona fide adult VP is best described as limited/unknown.
- **Stimulus:** Touch (trigger hair), heat (>~38°C), cold, glutamate, and electrical stimulation → AP. Critically, wounding also → AP (not VP) in mature traps. Only immature stage-V traps show a slow-wave/VP-like response; this is a demonstrated developmental effect, not assumed.
- *Cite:* Pavlovič A, Jakšová J & Novák O (2017) 'Triggering a false alarm: wounding mimics prey capture in the carnivorous Venus flytrap (Dionaea muscipula)' New Phytol. 216:927–938 (wounding and trigger-hair stimulation induce the SAME action potentials); Scherzer S, Böhm J, Huang S, Iosip AL, ... Hedrich R (2022) 'A unique inventory of ion transporters poises the Venus flytrap to fast-propagating action potentials and calcium waves' Curr. Biol. 32(19):4356–4362 (stage-V trap wounding → slow-wave potential vs stage-VI trap → fast AP); Hodick D & Sievers A (1988) 'The action potential of Dionaea muscipula Ellis' Planta 174:8–18; Böhm J et al. (2016) Curr. Biol. 26:286–295 (AP counting); Burdon-Sanderson J (1873) Proc. R. Soc. Lond. 21:495–496 (first AP recording).

## Higher (vascular) plants — the general case: both AP and VP
- **AP:** yes — demonstrated in many mono- and dicots (e.g. sunflower, pea, wheat, tomato, maize, pumpkin); AP is a fast, all-or-none, self-propagating depolarization travelling in phloem/vascular parenchyma
- **VP:** yes — demonstrated in the same species; VP (a.k.a. slow-wave potential) is a slower, decremental, variable-amplitude, non-all-or-none depolarization propagated hydraulically via the xylem (plus a chemical/ROS component)
- **Stimulus:** Non-damaging stimuli (cold, mechanical touch, electrical current, change in illumination, mild salt) -> AP; damaging/wounding stimuli (flame/burn, local heating to high temperature, crushing, cutting, organ excision) -> VP. VP requires the pressure/hydraulic surge in the xylem that damage produces.
- *Cite:* Fromm & Lautner 2007, Plant, Cell & Environment 30:249-257; Vodeneev, Akinchits & Sukhov 2015, Plant Signaling & Behavior 10(9):e1057365

## "Do ALL higher plants produce both?" — general feature, widespread but NOT proven universal (demonstrated vs assumed)
- **AP:** likely/general — AP capability shown across many monocots and dicots, not just specialized (Mimosa/Dionaea) plants; regarded as a general physiological feature rather than the exception, but not every angiosperm species has been tested, so universality is an inference, not a proof
- **VP:** likely/general — VP similarly demonstrated across diverse vascular species; treated as general to plants possessing xylem, but 'every higher plant produces VP' is an extrapolation from the tested set, not established for all species
- **Stimulus:** The non-damaging->AP / damaging->VP dichotomy is the general rule the reviews assert as widespread; the claim of universality across all higher plants is an assumption of shared physiology, not an exhaustive demonstration.
- *Cite:* Trebacz, Dziubinska & Krol 2006, 'Electrical Signals in Long-Distance Communication in Plants,' in Communication in Plants (Baluska, Mancuso & Volkmann eds.), Springer, pp. 277-290; Fromm & Lautner 2007, Plant, Cell & Environment 30:249-257

## Non-vascular plants and green algae — AP-only (VP absent because there is no xylem)
- **AP:** yes — action potentials well documented in charophyte algae (Chara, Nitella, Nitellopsis internodal cells) fired by depolarization, light, touch, cold, heat-shock or injury, and in the liverwort Conocephalum conicum and mosses in response to light/touch/electrical stimuli
- **VP:** no (VP is not generated) — VP/slow-wave potential is explicitly described as a hydraulically propagated signal UNIQUE to higher (vascular) plants; algae, liverworts and mosses lack the xylem needed to generate a wound-induced hydraulic VP, so these groups are effectively AP-only
- **Stimulus:** AP evoked by both non-damaging (light, touch, cold, electrical) and even injurious stimuli, but the response is an AP, not a distinct VP; the wound signal does not take the xylem-hydraulic VP form seen in vascular plants.
- *Cite:* Beilby 2007, 'Action potential in charophytes,' International Review of Cytology 257:43-82; Trebacz, Dziubinska & Krol 2006 (Springer, Communication in Plants); Stahlberg, Cleland & Van Volkenburgh 2006, 'Slow wave potentials — a propagating electrical signal unique to higher plants,' in Communication in Plants, Springer, pp. 291-308

## Sensitive / carnivorous plants (Mimosa pudica, Dionaea muscipula, Aldrovanda, Drosera) — classic AP producers from non-damaging touch
- **AP:** yes — rapid leaf/trap/tentacle movements are triggered by touch-evoked action potentials in motor cells (Mimosa AP likely a Cl- spike; Dionaea/Aldrovanda AP a Ca2+ spike); these were the first plant APs recorded (late 19th c.)
- **VP:** yes/likely (context-dependent) — these species are studied primarily for touch-evoked APs, but wounding of the same tissues can also elicit slower VP-type responses; VP is not the signal responsible for the fast movements
- **Stimulus:** Non-damaging mechanical touch (trigger-hair deflection, pulvinus stimulation) -> AP driving movement; wounding/heat -> VP. The behaviourally important signal here is the AP.
- *Cite:* Sibaoka 1991, 'Rapid plant movements triggered by action potentials,' Botanical Magazine (Tokyo) / Journal of Plant Research 104:73-95; Hedrich 2023, 'Demystifying the Venus flytrap action potential,' New Phytologist

## Same species produces BOTH, distinguished by stimulus — sunflower (Helianthus annuus) as the worked example
- **AP:** yes — electrical or cold stimulation evokes a fast, all-or-none, constant-velocity AP
- **VP:** yes — flame/burn wounding evokes a VP that is slower, variable in shape/amplitude, decrements with distance, and is tied to xylem tension/turgor-pressure surges (externally applied pressure mimics the burn-induced VP)
- **Stimulus:** Direct demonstration in one species that non-damaging electrical/cold -> AP while wound/burn -> VP, with the two signals shown to be mechanistically distinct (all-or-none electrical vs hydraulic/decremental).
- *Cite:* Stankovic, Zawadzki & Davies 1997, 'Characterization of the Variation Potential in Sunflower,' Plant Physiology 115(3):1083-1088; Stankovic, Witters, Zawadzki & Davies 1998, 'Action potentials and variation potentials in sunflower...,' Physiologia Plantarum 103(1):51-58

## Stimulus->signal mapping is NOT absolute — documented species-specific exceptions
- **AP:** yes, but the eliciting stimulus can differ by species — e.g. heating (normally a VP inducer) has been reported to evoke APs in some plants (e.g. bean, potato), so the 'non-damaging=AP' rule has exceptions
- **VP:** yes — VP dominates after damaging stimuli in most species, but which stimulus crosses into VP territory, and VP amplitude/velocity, are species- and stimulus-specific (parameters differ for wounding vs burning vs high-temperature heating)
- **Stimulus:** The AP<->non-damaging and VP<->damaging correspondence is a strong general tendency, not a law; the type, intensity and speed of the stimulus, and the species, determine which signal appears, and mixed AP+VP responses occur.
- *Cite:* Mudrilov, Ladeynova, Grinberg, Balalaeva & Vodeneev 2021, 'Electrical Signaling of Plants under Abiotic Stressors,' International Journal of Molecular Sciences 22(19):10715; Mudrilov, Ladeynova, Vetrova & Vodeneev 2024, 'Analysis of the Mechanisms Underlying the Specificity of the Variation Potential Induced by Different Stimuli,' Plants 13(20):2896

## Functional endpoint — wound-induced VP (and AP) drives systemic gene expression (tomato); note the historical AP-vs-VP identity debate
- **AP:** yes — the wound-propagated electrical signal in tomato can include an AP component
- **VP:** yes — the systemic wound signal in tomato was subsequently characterized as (largely) a VP; both AP and VP were shown able to induce proteinase-inhibitor (pin) gene expression, so the identity of the original 'wound signal' was debated (Wildon's signal reinterpreted as VP)
- **Stimulus:** Wounding/heat -> VP (± AP) -> systemic defense gene induction; a landmark demonstrating a physiological consequence of the wound-evoked electrical signal.
- *Cite:* Wildon et al. 1992, 'Electrical signalling and systemic proteinase inhibitor induction in the wounded plant,' Nature 360:62-65; Stankovic & Davies 1996, 'Both action potentials and variation potentials induce proteinase inhibitor gene expression in tomato,' FEBS Letters 390(3):275-279

## General AP-vs-VP framework (context; definitions and stimulus dependence)
- **AP:** AP = fast, all-or-none, non-decremental, propagates via refractory-limited ion fluxes (Ca2+ in, Cl-/K+ out); elicited by NON-damaging stimuli (cold/chilling, touch, light, mild electrical)
- **VP:** VP (slow wave potential) = slower, variable waveform, DECREMENTAL, hydraulic/xylem-pressure-driven; elicited by DAMAGING stimuli (cutting, wounding, burning/heat)
- **Stimulus:** chilling/touch → AP; cutting/burning/heat → VP, demonstrated directly in maize leaf phloem
- *Cite:* Fromm & Lautner 2007, Plant Cell & Environment 30(3):249-257 (PMID 17263772); Fromm, Hajirezaei, Becker & Lautner 2013, Frontiers in Plant Science 4:239 (PMC3701874); review 'Electrical Signaling of Plants under Abiotic Stressors: Transmission of Stimulus-Specific Information', Int J Mol Sci 2021, 22(19):10715, doi:10.3390/ijms221910715
