# Do signal-transformation properties track evolutionary relationship?

Comparative analysis over 13 species (165 valid recordings). The three
distance-independent metrics — amplitude attenuation (far/near peak), temporal
broadening (far/near FWHM), and waveform similarity (max cross-correlation) —
are used as per-species functional traits (species medians). Generated by
`scripts/comparative.py`; figures in `results/figures/`.

> **Bounding caveats.** 13 species with only two replicated families
> (Lamiaceae n=5, Solanaceae n=3) → low power. Read the conclusions as
> exploratory, not confirmatory. (The geometry-confound worry is addressed in
> §4: with distances now known, attenuation and broadening turn out to be
> uncorrelated with electrode spacing.)

## 1. The transformation is **not** phylogenetically structured

- **Mantel test** (functional distance vs taxonomic distance): Spearman
  r = −0.02, p = 0.50. No association.
- **Kruskal–Wallis across families** (species medians, Lamiaceae vs Solanaceae):
  attenuation p = 0.30, broadening p = 0.65, similarity p = 0.65. No family
  effect survives.
- **Congeners diverge as much as strangers.** The two *Capsicum* (Chilean vs
  Ornamental Chile) are among the most *different* pairs in the whole set —
  attenuation 0.63 vs 0.19, broadening 1.13 vs 1.87, similarity 0.86 vs 0.69.
  (These are the only true congeners in the set — *Capsicum baccatum* and
  *C. annuum* — and they are among the most divergent pairs.) Mint (*Mentha
  spicata*) and Hierbabuena (*Clinopodium douglasii*) are same-family, different-
  genus Lamiaceae and likewise differ in broadening (1.45 vs 1.02) and shape
  fidelity (0.68 vs 0.85).
- **Dendrogram** (`functional_dendrogram.png`): family colours are scattered
  across the tree, not clustered. Basil (Lamiaceae) groups with a monocot
  (Creeping Inchplant); Chilean Chile (Solanaceae) groups with Venus flytrap
  (Droseraceae).

**Conclusion:** at the family/genus level sampled here, how a plant reshapes a
propagating electrical signal is not inherited. Relatedness does not predict it.

## 2. The dominant axis is a decremental ↔ regenerative conduction continuum

This axis is *mechanistic and convergent*, which is why it cuts across taxonomy.

- **Attenuation and broadening co-vary** (`attenuation_vs_broadening.png`):
  Spearman ρ ≈ −0.26 between amplitude-ratio and width-ratio — signals that
  lose amplitude also spread out (top-left cluster: strong decrement + strong
  broadening), while signals that keep their amplitude keep their width
  (bottom-right). This is the fingerprint of passive, dissipative (low-pass /
  cable-like) conduction at one extreme versus faithful, regenerative
  conduction at the other.
- **Broadening degrades shape fidelity**: broadening vs similarity ρ = −0.37,
  p = 8×10⁻⁷. Dispersion is *the* thing that distorts the waveform.
- **The "regenerative" end is occupied by the rapid-movement plants.** Venus
  flytrap and Sensitive Mimosa have the highest waveform similarity (≈0.90–0.93),
  amplitude that is preserved rather than decremented (median far/near ≈ 0.56 and
  ≈1.03), and the shortest inter-channel delays. These are the textbook
  action-potential plants — and they sit in *different* families (Droseraceae,
  Fabaceae). Fast, shape-preserving signalling is **convergent**, tied to the
  need to trigger rapid movement, not to common ancestry.

## 3. Within-species variability is the single largest source of variance

The per-recording scatter within each species (dots in
`near_to_far_by_species.png`) spans much of the total range, and the IQR bars
are wide. Species identity explains less than the recording-to-recording spread.
The likely drivers are physiological state and geometry — electrode spacing and
contact, wound severity, hydration, stimulus type — i.e. the biophysics of each
individual conduction event, not the label on the pot.

## 4. Conduction velocity (all species, distances from the spreadsheet)

Electrode distances for 166/176 recordings were cross-referenced from the
*Todo Data Resumen* spreadsheet (`scripts/extract_distances.py` →
`data/distances.csv`; 10 unmatched because those recording dates are absent from
the sheet).

> **Channel integrity (common-mode is expected, not a defect).** Both electrodes
> share a soil/earth reference, so a large *instantaneous* common component is
> normal (see `docs/active_vs_passive.md` §0b). It does **not** prevent recovering
> the delay: a common-mode-robust 2-tap model — far = a·near(t) + b·near(t−τ) —
> separates the shared part (a) from the delayed propagation (b), and recovers a
> real, sign-consistent delay for essentially all recordings (delayed-fraction
> 0.68–0.90; 151/165 resolved). An earlier over-aggressive time-floor wrongly
> flagged the fast species; the CV of **Venus flytrap (~28 mm/s)** is genuine —
> a small electrode spacing on a fast, Ca²⁺-rich trap gives a small *but real*
> delay plus strong volume-conduction correlation, not "no delay."

Median CV (resolved-delay recordings), slowest to fastest:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Argentian Dollar | 2.1 | | Creeping Inchplant | 6.7 |
| Rosemary | 3.8 | | Tomato | 8.4 |
| Mint | 3.9 | | Ruda | 9.8 |
| Hierbabuena | 4.0 | | Basil | 11.0 |
| Ornamental Chile | 5.6 | | Sensitive Mimosa | 12.8 |
| Marijuana | 5.8 | | Chilean Chile | 13.6 |
| | | | **Venus flytrap** | **28.1** |

The pipeline reports three CV columns per recording (`recordings.csv`):
`cv_xcorr_mm_s` (default cross-correlation), `cv_2tap` (common-mode-robust 2-tap
delay), and `cv_manual` (distance ÷ the experimenters' own spreadsheet delay).
They agree for every species except Mimosa's fast July batch; `cv_manual`
reproduces the paper exactly (Mimosa 30.6, Venus 35.7 mm/s) and is the column to
use when matching the manuscript numbers.

A forward-simulation check (`scripts/sim_vs_real.py`): predicting the far trace
from the near trace, the **active model (delayed copy + shared component)
out-predicts the passive cable** for almost every species (median R² 0.4–0.87 vs
0.2–0.79), and the fitted delays are sign-consistent — i.e. the far trace really
is a delayed copy of the near trace, not a dispersed one.

All values sit in the earlier hand-measured 1–40 mm/s range. Venus flytrap is
the clear outlier (fast action potentials); Sensitive Mimosa is also fast.

**Three independent validations come out of having the distances:**
1. **Our delay vs the experimenters' manual delay** (`delay_validation.png`):
   Spearman ρ = 0.80 (n = 154), points on the identity line — and the
   order-inferred matches agree as well as the exact ones, so the cross-
   reference is sound *and* the automatic delay reproduces the manual one.
2. **Delay scales with electrode distance** (ρ = +0.36, p = 3×10⁻⁶): the lag is
   genuine propagation, not an artifact.
3. **CV is independent of the spacing used** (ρ = +0.03, ns): a real velocity
   should not depend on how far apart the electrodes were placed — and it does
   not.

A useful side effect: attenuation and broadening are **uncorrelated with
distance** (ρ ≈ 0), so the cross-species differences in §2 are *not* a
spacing/geometry artifact after all — they reflect the conduction itself.

### Does CV track taxonomy?

Weakly at best, and not significantly. **Kruskal–Wallis of CV across families**
(Lamiaceae vs Solanaceae): p = 0.10 — the mint family trends slow (Argentian
Dollar 2.1, Rosemary 3.8, Mint 3.9, Hierbabuena 4.0) but **Basil (also
Lamiaceae) is fast at 11.0**, breaking the pattern. **Congeneric pairs are
inconsistent**: the two *Mentha* mints have essentially identical CV
(3.90 vs 3.97), yet the two *Capsicum* chiles differ 2.4-fold (13.6 vs 5.6). So
even CV — the most biologically meaningful trait — is not cleanly predicted by
relatedness, reinforcing §1.

## What one could do next

- **Record electrode spacing for every recording** (`-<mm>mm` in the filename)
  to (a) unlock absolute CV for all species and (b) remove the spacing confound
  from attenuation/broadening, making the cross-species comparison clean.
- **Test the decremental↔regenerative axis against physiological covariates**
  (stem diameter, turgor/hydration, stimulus modality and intensity) rather than
  taxonomy — the data say that is where the signal lives.
- **Separate signal classes explicitly**: classify each event as
  variation-potential-like vs action-potential-like (from attenuation +
  similarity + delay) before pooling, since Venus/Mimosa are a different regime.
- **Grow the phylogenetic sample** (more species per family, and a real
  branch-length phylogeny with Blomberg's K / Pagel's λ) before any firm claim
  about phylogenetic signal — the current null is suggestive, not conclusive.
